## 3. The two attacks

**The number step** (`docs/07-auth.md` 12.14 a, `DEC-2`). Decided: an unknown number answers
exactly like a real account with a PIN, and its PIN step, "Forgot PIN" and code step are
simulated from the sign-in history; only numbers of people without a PIN, with a forced reset
pending, or blocked answer differently, as the flow requires. Residual risk: those three
groups can be told apart by an outsider, and probing the first two mails a capped code.

**Lock-by-stranger** (12.14 b, `DEC-1`). Decided: the lock is per person across devices in
two scopes; trusted-device failures lock every device, untrusted-device failures lock new
devices, both until a code. Argued: the literal rule lets anyone lock a person everywhere in
five taps, and its "reset on a correct PIN" lets a stranger guess four times per sign-in of
the owner without ever locking; per-device and per-address limits cannot stop a five-try
attack. Residual risk: a stranger can still lock a person out of new devices; a thief with
the trusted phone can lock every device; ten guesses in all for an attacker with both kinds
of device. The owner may choose the literal single lock (question 47).

Both are recorded in `docs/12-security.md` 2.7 and 7, with three more decided threats
(mail flood, a stolen device token, a copied v2.11 device id) and the device-name phishing
risk found by the challenge pass.

### 6.3 Conceded challenges (the guard that holds)

- A holder of a six-digit PIN typing on the new web (which submits on the fourth digit)
  locks themselves "on every device": cannot happen, because a new web device is untrusted
  until a first sign-in succeeds there, so its wrong PINs count in the new-devices scope;
  their phone keeps working and "Forgot PIN" ends it (`DEC-1`, `DEC-7`).
- Two target saves on one vehicle racing: both lock the vehicle row first (`docs/05` 10.4);
  the vehicle update takes the same row, then the organization row, in the same order.
- Legacy keys could let an unmoved routine pass for someone who should not: a legacy key is
  held only through its sources, which are the rights that replace it, so an unmoved routine
  admits exactly the people the new rights admit (and keeps working between D2 and D3/D4).
- The back-dating limit leaking through a 403 order: the limit is checked after the
  permission, so a person without the right never learns it (`docs/05` 10.2).
- A replay of an offline capture refused by the limit: the identical-replay step precedes
  every check (`docs/05` 10.5).
